# User lens

Goal: the result matches what the people it serves actually expect, including what they didn't say.

## Procedure
1. **Identify the people:**
   - **Requester:** who is asking, their role and skill level, how they will use your output (ship it, learn from it, present it).
   - **End users:** who uses the result, their context (device, skill, urgency, accessibility needs).
2. **Infer unstated expectations** for that audience. Typical ones: it works on first try, looks finished, handles empty/error states, is fast, is secure, is easy to change later, matches the existing style.
3. **Name the judging criteria:** the 2–3 things this audience will notice first, and what would disappoint them.
4. **Apply it:** let these criteria drive scope, defaults, and priorities. Cut what this audience won't value.
5. **Pitch the explanation** to the requester's level: beginners get the why and the steps; experts get the decision and the tradeoff.

## Failure handling
- Audience is unknown and it changes the result: ask once (see `clarify`).
- Requester's expectation conflicts with end users' needs: surface the conflict with a recommendation; don't silently pick.

## Output
Who it's for (requester, end users) · what they expect · how they will judge it · decisions this changed.
